ABOUT

EQUALLY AT HOME AT BOTH ENDS OF THE PIPELINE.

Most analytical scientists plant their flag firmly on one side of a divide — either the intellectual freedom of early discovery, or the disciplined rigour of regulated development. I’ve spent 14 years doing both, and I’ve never quite managed to choose between them. That, as it turns out, is rather useful.

My doctorate at the University of Sheffield trained me to think carefully, write precisely, and never present a result without understanding where it might go wrong — a habit that has served me well ever since. The postdoctoral years at the University of Nebraska produced what I’d consider my most ambitious piece of work: a complete phenotypic drug discovery campaign from scratch — 43,783 compounds screened, 243 hits confirmed, mechanism deconvoluted by integrated multi-omics, and a US patent granted. Not bad for a plant biologist, all things considered.

The move into industry brought a different kind of challenge. As Principal Investigator at Thermo Fisher Scientific (PPD), I now lead 16 GxP-regulated LC-MS analytical programmes for five global clients, from preclinical candidate selection through Phase III. The science is different; the need to get it right first time is exactly the same.

“The best analytical scientists I’ve known could tell you not just what the data said, but what it meant for the programme. That’s the kind of scientist I’ve tried to be.”

I’m currently open to consulting engagements, advisory roles, and senior full-time positions. If you have a tricky analytical problem — whether it’s a method that won’t behave, a screen that needs designing properly, or a dataset that’s produced a heatmap rather than an answer — I’d be genuinely keen to hear about it.

SIGNATURE WORK

A COMPLETE DRUG DISCOVERY CAMPAIGN. PUBLISHED IN A PLANT JOURNAL.

Two Plant Physiology papers document what is, in terms of mechanistic scope and experimental rigour, a full pharma-equivalent phenotypic drug discovery campaign — from first-principles HTS platform design through cheminformatics-driven SAR, integrated multi-omics mechanism of action, and a granted US patent. The biological context is algae. The workflow is, to put it plainly, indistinguishable from a modern pharmaceutical programme. Draw your own conclusions about why it ended up in a plant biology journal.

01

HTS PLATFORM DESIGN

Nile Red/OD dual-readout assay built from scratch and properly validated — Z′ = 0.68 in a 1,717-compound NCI pilot, then scaled to a 43,783-compound full campaign at Z′ = 0.78 across 124 × 384-well plates. Classified “excellent assay” at pilot stage.

02

HIT VALIDATION & DOSE-RESPONSE

243 confirmed hits across 5 structural scaffolds. 4PL/Hill equation in R, EC₅₀/therapeutic index ranking. Brefeldin A confirmed at EC₅₀ = 2.59 µM. Orthogonal validation: GC-MS FAME, HPTLC, confocal microscopy, western blot (DGAT2A). No short cuts.

03

SAR BY CHEMINFORMATICS

ChemMine Tanimoto clustering, Cytoscape ChemViz, ChemAxon in TIBCO Spotfire. Structural determinants of selective lipid induction vs. cytotoxic liability — a proper MPO lead optimisation analysis in all but name.

04

MULTI-OMICS MoA DECONVOLUTION

TMT proteomics (884 proteins) + RNA-seq (15,965 genes) + LC-MS metabolomics (11,752 features), integrated into a single mechanistic study. Target axis: ACLY → citrate flux → DGAT2 → ARF1/Golgi trafficking. No ER stress, no lipotoxicity, no hand-waving.

WHY THIS IS RELEVANT TO YOUR PROGRAMME

  • ACLY — validated NASH/obesity target; bempedoic acid mechanism
  • DGAT1/2 — active drug discovery targets; DGAT2 inhibitors in Phase II/III
  • ARF1/vesicular trafficking — emerging target class in lipid storage disorders and metabolic oncology
  • The workflow is structurally identical to pharmaceutical phenotypic drug discovery — full stop

WHAT THE CLIENT GOT

  • $725,000 in external grant funding from two independent sources
